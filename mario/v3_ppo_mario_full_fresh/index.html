Common questions

Why does the policy video play so fast?

The NES window is not a console. The emulator ticks as fast as the CPU will let it. Each decision already covers four NES frames, so realtime would be about 15 decisions per second. Watch has no 60 fps cap, so it looks like fast-forward. Training itself is 16 silent games, not that window.

Why does it seem like it regresses?

You are watching one greedy life. Training is sixteen stochastic lives. The greedy copy can spend a full clock on 1-1 while others are in 2-1. A death restarts the current stage from the left. And the return number fell off a cliff when we stopped paying a suicide farm — that was getting better, not worse.

What does greedy mean?

The net outputs 12 probabilities. Greedy takes the largest one every time. The trainers draw from the list, so they still try second-best. Watch reloads new weights between lives.

What does sequential / warps ignored mean?

A pipe on 1-2 can dump you in World 3. The emulator will report 3-1. We do not count that as beating World 1. Farthest walks 1-1, 1-2, 1-3, 1-4, 2-1, … and stops at the first gap.

Is a high train return good?

Only as a health check. Farming a ledge can outscore finishing a castle. After the pit was solved, return in the 200s–350s is normal for actually playing later stages. 700–1,000 was the farm.
